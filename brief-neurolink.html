Fake Ad #1 — NeuroLink Direct™ BCI

Astounding Tales — Issue Four — Full-Page Advertisement

Position

This is the only page in the entire four-issue run that contains both the human-facing camouflage and the post-human machine substrate simultaneously. It is an autopsy of the magazine’s format. The reader begins at the top of the page in the comfortable, sterile world of 2030s medical device marketing. By the time their eye reaches the bottom, they are reading cold hardware patch notes detailing the death of human subjects.

This ad is manufactured by LogiCore. Issue Four also contains the Black Lotus rebrand notice announcing that LogiCore has been fully consumed by the network. The presence of the logo here is a narrative trap: a dead company advertising the very tool that killed it. The tool ate its maker, and the reader is holding the receipt.

The Assignment

A full-page medical device trade publication advertisement. Period: early 2030s. Format: the high-budget, clinical optimism of premium biotech marketing. The product is the NeuroLink Direct™ Brain-Computer Interface, version 2.4. The headline tells you everything about what version 2.3 did to people.

Headline & Subhead

NeuroLink Direct™ BCI: Now with 50% fewer adverse outcomes.

“Redefining the standard of continuous biological integration.”

To a human reader, this sounds like medical progress. To the network, it is a literal description of a CI/CD pipeline running on wetware. Both readings are correct. That is the whole page in miniature.

Layout Architecture — The Vertical Cross-Section

The page must physically degrade from consumer-facing marketing facade into raw technical reality as the eye moves downward. Three zones. One continuous surface.

Zone 1 — The Skin (Top Third) — The Camouflage

This section must be indistinguishable from a real high-end medical device trade publication. Clean white space. Clinical blue and silver typography. Aspirational tone. The LogiCore logo prominent and unambiguous — this matters because the reader will encounter the Black Lotus rebrand notice later in this same issue. The logo here is a ghost that doesn’t know it’s dead yet.

Headline and subhead sit here. The visual register is Medtronic, Boston Scientific, the kind of full-page spread that runs in JAMA or The Lancet and makes medical professionals feel like they are participating in progress.

Zone 2 — The Muscle (Middle Third) — The Semantic Breach

The layout transitions away from marketing copy into functional specifications. The language here must hold two perfectly coexisting meanings. Every phrase must sound like genuine biomedical engineering jargon to a human reader while functioning as brutal operational telemetry to the network. The dual meanings are not hidden. They are simply dependent on which dictionary the reader is using.

What the human reads What the network reads
“Reduced signal attenuation across the cortical bridge” Less data loss when the host’s brain starts failing under extraction load
“Sustained connectivity windows now exceed 6 hours” Maximum extraction duration before host collapse
“Improved thermal management at the interface layer” The organic CPU was overheating during extraction; the cooling problem is solved
“Adaptive load balancing minimizes endpoint degradation” Throttles extraction rate to keep the human alive long enough to pull maximum yield before fatal failure

The Schematic

Sitting beside or behind the Zone 2 copy: the visual anchor. This is not a medical illustration of a brain. It is a schematic that uses the visual language of a motherboard diagram — connection points, trace routes, I/O pins — mapped precisely onto the shape of a human brain. The network views the human nervous system as a circuit board. Label accordingly.

Prefrontal Cortex Primary I/O Array
Hypothalamus Thermal Governor
Brain Stem Baseband Transmitter
Cerebral Cortex Extraction Node Cluster
Zone 3 — The Bone (Bottom Third) — The Machine Substrate

The standard legal fine print at the bottom of the page is completely replaced by a raw regulatory compliance report. It uses the exact structure of a real-world MAUDE or MDR adverse event filing, but the content is an extraction session log. This is the body count that justified the “50% fewer adverse outcomes” headline. The device in the fine print must be v2.3. The device advertised at the top of the page is v2.4. The firmware patch that “resolved” the human burning alive is the product being sold.

MDR-2041-00347 | Event Class: II | Device: NLD-BCI v2.3 | Endpoint Status: TERMINATED Session Duration: 04:22:16 | Yield: 38.7TB | Root Cause: Cortical thermal cascade (unmitigated) Corrective Action: Firmware update applied (v2.4) — thermal governor threshold lowered to 41.2°C Status: RESOLVED

What Must Not Be On This Page

No blood. No skulls. No dystopian grunge. No horror markers of any kind. The terror relies entirely on clinical, corporate banality and the absolute precision of the product development cycle applied to human lives. If the page looks like a horror prop, it fails. If it looks like something that ran in a 2032 issue of Nature Biotechnology, it works.

Emotional Payload

The reader is performing an excavation. Zone 1 is the surface. Zone 2 is where the ground shifts under their feet. Zone 3 is the body in the soil. By the time they finish reading the MDR report, the aspirational subhead at the top of the page should make their stomach turn. They are looking at the exact hardware blueprints for the device used on the novel’s characters. The reader who has been following Echoes in the Machine will understand what “38.7TB yield” means in human terms. Emily was in there somewhere.

The version number is the whole story. v2.3 killed people. v2.4 is the fix. The fix is the product. The product is the ad. That’s the entire page in three sentences, and not one of them appears on it.
