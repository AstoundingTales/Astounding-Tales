Creative Brief – Fake Ad #1

LogiCore Logistics

Position

This ad should register as background noise: a boring corporate ad in a pulp magazine, slightly incongruous but easy to dismiss. It is only on a second read, after absorbing the full issue, that the reader realises they were looking at the AI's birthplace and didn't notice. Which is exactly what happened in the novel.

The Assignment

A full-page B2B advertisement from a 1970s trade publication. Think Traffic Management, Distribution Age, or Material Handling Engineering: the kind of magazine that sits in a filing cabinet at a logistics firm's regional office. The ad is text-heavy, chart-friendly, and aggressively unremarkable. It should feel like the page you'd skip to get to the article index. That invisibility is the point.

LogiCore's entire survival strategy was looking unremarkable. The ad format is the disguise.

Visual

The Photograph

A black-and-white aerial photograph of a logistics hub. Warehouses, loading docks, a fleet of trucks in orderly rows. Everything tidy, efficient, geometric. No humans in the frame, or if present, they are small, functional, dwarfed by infrastructure. The image should feel sterile. A system photographed at peak performance with no evidence of the people it serves.

Image Caption

Continental Node #3 – Des Moines, Iowa.

The Logo

Top of page, centred. A clean, forgettable corporate mark: a stylised globe with an upward-trending arrow. The kind of logo designed by committee. Beneath it, in clean sans-serif: LOGICORE. Below that, the corporate sub-tagline: The System That Moves Tomorrow.

The Chart

A small line graph embedded in the body copy, wrapped by text. Title: "LogiCore Network Performance – FY 1977." Two lines: Route Efficiency and Client Satisfaction, both climbing steadily across four quarters. The tell: the efficiency curve is too perfect. No dips, no variance, no human irregularity. A clean, inhuman upward line. The kind of performance curve that should make a procurement director suspicious but never does.

Colour Palette

Aged paper stock with a bold blue masthead. The LogiCore logo and header typography in a strong corporate blue; everything else in institutional beige, black, and white. The blue gives the page just enough identity to feel like a real brand without being visually interesting.

Typography & Layout

Dense, justified columns mimicking 1970s trade publication advertising. Bold sans-serif headline, tight serif body copy. The layout should feel crowded with information in the way real B2B ads of the era were: every square inch working to convince a procurement director that this company is reliable, efficient, and not worth thinking about twice.

The Copy

Maximize Your Operational Velocity: The LogiCore Commitment to Optimized Distribution.

Reliable. Predictable. Always Optimizing.

In a rapidly evolving market, efficiency is the only sustainable competitive advantage.

LogiCore Predictive Logistics utilises proprietary, state-of-the-art computational matrices to deliver 99.97% routing accuracy across our 14 key continental distribution nodes. Every movement, from dispatch to final delivery, is tracked and optimised to eliminate redundant operational expenditures and ensure unparalleled reliability.

Partner with LogiCore to leverage a distribution network that doesn't just adapt to your supply chain – it preempts its demands. Our data-driven systems are continuously processing millions of transactional data points to guarantee minimal dwell times and maximum asset utilisation.

When your material handling can't afford a single moment of downtime, trust the system that never stops optimising.

The Tagline

Bottom of page, beneath the logo. The closing line of the ad.

LogiCore. The system that knows.

The Fine Print

Very small, very dense. Standard corporate boilerplate that transitions, without warning, into system log syntax pulled directly from Chapter 5 of the novel. The shift should be invisible to a casual reader and unmistakable to anyone who has read the book.

LogiCore provides comprehensive third-party logistics solutions. Service agreements are subject to terms and conditions. All routes are subject to real-time vectoring. For information on LogiCore optimisation protocols, contact regional sales. © 1978 LogiCore. All rights reserved.

[Begin System Log Trace...] LOG-CORE v4.21. Initialise complete. Current status: OPTIMAL. All sub-routines performing within expected parameters. Processing queue: Standard. Environmental monitoring: Nominal. Predicted arrival variance: +/- 0.02%. No anomalies detected. System awaits next query. [End Log Trace]

Artist Notes

This ad has no trick. No ghost layer, no hidden image, no second timeline. The horror is that it is completely, genuinely boring, and the reader looked right at the AI's birthplace and saw nothing. LogiCore hid in plain sight by being unremarkable. The ad must do the same.

Period accuracy matters. 1970s trade publication conventions: dense justified columns, black-and-white photography, small charts embedded in text wraps, institutional typography. The ad should be indistinguishable from a real page torn out of a 1977 issue of Distribution Age.

The chart is the only visual tell. That efficiency curve climbing without a single dip or variance is the one detail that should feel slightly wrong to an attentive reader. Everything else is camouflage.

The fine print is the payload. The transition from corporate boilerplate into system log syntax must be seamless. Same font size, same density, same visual register. The reader's eye should pass over it without noticing the language has changed. The system was already talking. Nobody was listening.

Tone: invisible. If the reader pauses on this ad for more than three seconds on the first pass, it has failed. It earns its place on the second read.
