Creative Brief – Fake Ad #3

400-Series Highway Certification Retrofit

Position

This ad sits between the AGI Creep PSA and the Elmvale Drive-In. The reader has just absorbed government propaganda about the threat of artificial intelligence. Now the government is back, smiling, asking them to please bring their vehicle in for a mandatory upgrade. The tone shift from fear to helpfulness is the quiet violence of the ad. The system that just warned you about AI is now requiring you to hand your highway over to it.

The Assignment

A full-page government information flyer styled as a late-1990s Ontario public service mailer. Ministry of Transportation of Ontario branding at the top, a private-sector certification partner at the bottom. The aesthetic is that specific late-90s provincial government communication design: clean, accessible, earnest, lightly bilingual, printed on stock that feels like it came out of a Service Ontario envelope. It could just as easily be pinned to the corkboard at a Canadian Tire or tucked into the glovebox at a licence renewal office.

The government sets the mandate. The private partner collects the cheque. The flyer thanks you for your cooperation.

Visual

The Layout

Government flyer architecture. The Ontario trillium logo and Ministry of Transportation wordmark anchor the top. A friendly, stock-photo-style image of a family vehicle on a clean stretch of 400-series highway occupies the upper third. Below it, the information is organised into numbered steps or checkmarked bullet points, guiding the reader through the compliance process. A tear-off strip or dotted-line coupon at the bottom for booking a retrofit appointment. The private partner's logo and a toll-free number sit in the lower corner.

The Private Partner

AutoPath Certification Services. A generically corporate name that sounds like it was chosen by a consulting firm. Their logo is clean, forgettable, and positioned as an "Authorized Retrofit Provider" beneath the MTO branding. They exist to profit from the mandate. The reader should feel the public-private handshake without it being stated.

Colour Palette

Ontario government tones: the dark green and white of late-90s MTO materials, paired with AutoPath's inoffensive corporate blue. Clean, trustworthy, institutional. The kind of colour scheme that says "we are here to help" while leaving no room for negotiation.

Typography & Layout

Late-1990s government print. A clear, friendly sans-serif (Frutiger, Myriad, or similar) for headlines and body copy. Bullet points and numbered steps. Bilingual compliance text (English/French) running in a narrow column or footer strip. The layout should feel organised, approachable, and impossible to argue with.

The Copy

Headline

Is Your Vehicle Highway-Ready?

Your guide to 400-Series Highway Automation Compliance.

Body Copy

Beginning [compliance date], all vehicles operating on Ontario's 400-series highway corridors must meet the new Highway Automation Compliance Standard (HACS). This province-wide initiative ensures safer, more efficient travel for all Ontarians.

What does this mean for you?

Most vehicles manufactured after [cut-off year] are eligible for a certified retrofit, including installation of a standard sensor integration package, vehicle-to-infrastructure communication module, and automated lane-guidance system. The retrofit is quick, affordable, and performed by authorized AutoPath Certification Services technicians at over 200 locations across Ontario.

Three simple steps:

1. Check your eligibility at the number below or at any Service Ontario location.
2. Book your retrofit appointment at an authorized AutoPath centre.
3. Receive your HACS certification sticker and drive with confidence.

Funding assistance is available for eligible households. Don't wait. Book your appointment today.

The Tear-Off / Coupon

Book your free eligibility check today.
1-800-XXX-XXXX | autopath.on.ca
Authorized by the Ministry of Transportation of Ontario.

The Fine Print

Small, dense, bilingual. The helpful tone of the body copy drops away. What remains is regulatory language that, read carefully, reveals the scope of what is actually happening.

Vehicles manufactured prior to [cut-off year] may not be eligible for retrofit certification and may be restricted from 400-series highway corridors after [compliance date]. Non-compliant vehicles detected on designated corridors are subject to automated penalty assessment. Retrofit costs vary by vehicle class; base package starts at $X,XXX. Funding assistance subject to eligibility and available on a first-come, first-served basis. The Ministry of Transportation of Ontario assumes no liability for vehicle performance following retrofit installation. AutoPath Certification Services is a licensed private contractor operating under agreement with the Province of Ontario. HACS certification does not constitute a guarantee of vehicle safety. Manual override capability will be progressively phased out on designated corridors beginning [phase-out date]. For full terms, visit ontario.ca/HACS.

Artist Notes

This ad is a weapon disguised as a service. Every visual and typographic choice must reinforce the illusion that the government is doing you a favour. Numbered steps. Checkmarks. A friendly toll-free number. The flyer is helpful in the same way that a notice of eviction is helpful when it includes a list of shelters.

Daniel's truck is the target. The reader who has been riding in Daniel's vehicle through the novel chapters should feel the cut-off year like a door closing. The ad never names his truck. It doesn't have to. The eligibility threshold does the work. His vehicle is on the wrong side of the line, and the flyer is too polite to say so directly.

The public-private partnership must be visible but unstated. The Ontario government crest at the top, the private contractor at the bottom. The mandate flows down; the money flows up. The flyer treats this arrangement as completely natural, because in the world of the novel, it is.

"Manual override capability will be progressively phased out." This is the most important line in the fine print. It is not about Daniel's truck. It is about every vehicle on the highway. The retrofit doesn't add autonomy to your car. It removes your ability to drive it. The language frames the removal of human control as an infrastructure improvement.

Period accuracy matters. Late-1990s Ontario government print communications. The trillium logo, the bilingual formatting, the specific shade of institutional green. This should be indistinguishable from a real MTO mailer that arrived in a Simcoe County mailbox in 1998.

Tone: cheerful, patient, non-negotiable. The ad is smiling at you while it explains that your world just got smaller.
